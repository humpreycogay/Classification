02

Concepts: fine-tuning vs training from scratch

This entry is fine-tuned, not trained from scratch. It starts from an encoder that has already learned grammar, word meanings and a lot of world knowledge from huge amounts of text. Our train split only has to teach it the labels. Pre-training is why it can learn the labels from a modest train split, and on both tasks here it scores above the networks trained from scratch (see the comparison below).

Training from scratch Fine-tuning
Starting weightsRandomPre-trained (ModernBERT has already read about 2 trillion tokens)

03

How fine-tuning works

How it works

diagram
my card has not arrived yet
        |  the model's own tokenizer (sub-word pieces, never "unknown")
[CLS] my card has not arrived yet [SEP]
        |  pre-trained encoder: every token attends to every other token,
        |  layer after layer (this is what fine-tuning adjusts, gently)
  one vector for the whole text
        |  new linear head (random at the start, one output per label)
  77 scores  ->  softmax(scores / T)  ->  card_arrival
  1. Load the pre-trained encoder at its pinned revision and put a new, randomly initialised linear layer on top, with one output per label.
  2. Tokenize each text with the model's own tokenizer and feed it through the encoder; the head reads the encoder's text vector.
  3. Cross-entropy loss against the true label; backpropagate through the head and the whole encoder.
  4. AdamW with a tiny learning rate (2e-5 to 5e-5, about a hundred times smaller than for the from-scratch networks), rising for the first 6% of steps and then falling to zero. Large steps would erase what pre-training taught: catastrophic forgetting.
  5. After each epoch, measure validation accuracy and keep the best epoch; then fit one temperature T so the probabilities are honest.

Steps 3 to 5 are the same loop the from-scratch networks use. Only the model, the tokenizer and the learning rate change.

04

The pre-trained backbone

ModelDeBERTa-v3-base (Microsoft, 2021)
Size184M parameters: 12 layers of hidden size 768 (86M) and a 128,000-token embedding table (98M); about 740 MB in float32
Pre-trainingELECTRA-style replaced-token detection on 160 GB of text
TokenizerSentencePiece, 128,000 sub-word tokens (spm.model): no unknown words
Revisionrefs/pr/14, Hugging Face's official SFconvertbot conversion of the original weights to safetensors. The main revision ships only a Python pickle (pytorch_model.bin), which this entry never downloads
Why itDisentangled attention (content and position kept apart) makes it one of the most accurate base-size encoders for classification; a second, independent backbone to compare with ModernBERT

Setup checks (both tasks): 184,425,220 parameters with the AG News head; fits 32 texts in 15 short epochs (1.000; 8 epochs were too few for 32 Banking77 labels with one text each); batch 1 vs 16 differ by at most 1.7e-06 in the logits; MPS vs CPU by at most 2.2e-06.

05

From text to tokens

Unlike the from-scratch networks, which build their own vocabulary from the train split, a fine-tuned model must use the tokenizer it was pre-trained with. It splits rare words into known pieces, so there is no "unknown word": a typo or a new product name still reaches the model as something it can read.

DeBERTa's tokenizer splits text into a similar number of pieces as ModernBERT's, so the same limits (160 and 64 tokens) keep nearly every text whole.

The benchmark's data preparation is unchanged: AG News text is {title}. {description}, Banking77 is the message. The test split is never used for training or for any choice.

Training procedure

Training

Fine-tuning runs automatically on the first benchmark run per task and is saved; later runs load it. On an M5 it takes minutes for Banking77 and over an hour for AG News, so run it plugged in.

bfloat16 training was checked against float32 on Banking77 before the real runs: both fit the 32-text check set perfectly after 15 epochs, so DeBERTa also trains with bfloat16 autocast.

Options for experiments (each trains and keeps a separate model and results folder): --train-limit 40000 (a quick pilot on part of the data), --train-seed 1 (another seed, to see the spread), --epochs, --lr and --fp32.

07

Reproducing it, step by step

  1. Set up the environment. Creates .venv, installs PyTorch and transformers, downloads the pinned backbone and runs the setup checks.
    shell
    ./run setup finetune_deberta
  2. Fine-tune and evaluate Banking77 first. It takes minutes and confirms the whole pipeline. The fine-tuned model is saved, and later runs load it.
    shell
    ./run finetune_deberta --task banking77 --n 1000
  3. Optionally, run a pilot on part of AG News to check the learning curve and the time per epoch before the full run. It is saved as a separate model with its own results folder.
    shell
    cd classifiers/finetune_deberta
PYTHONPATH=../../shared .venv/bin/python evaluate.py --task ag_news --train-limit 40000
  4. Fine-tune AG News. This takes over an hour on an Apple M5; use mains power.
    shell
    ./run finetune_deberta --task ag_news --n 1000
  5. Inspect the training. models/<task>/<digest>/meta.json holds the per-epoch training loss, validation loss and validation accuracy, the epoch kept and the temperature. Validation loss rising while training loss falls is the sign to stop.
  6. Check stability. Fine-tune again with another seed; each seed is saved separately, and a spread of about 0.1 point is normal.
    shell
    cd classifiers/finetune_deberta
PYTHONPATH=../../shared .venv/bin/python evaluate.py --task banking77 --n 0 --train-seed 1

08

The training loop in code

The heart of shared/finetune.py, shared by both fine-tuned entries:

python
for epoch in range(1, cfg.max_epochs + 1):
    model.train()
    for _, ids, mask, y in batches(fit_x, fit_y, cfg.batch_size, pad_id, rng):   # length-bucketed, shuffled
        with torch.autocast(device_type=device, dtype=torch.bfloat16, enabled=cfg.bf16):
            logits = model(input_ids=ids.to(device), attention_mask=mask.to(device)).logits
        loss = F.cross_entropy(logits.float(), y.to(device))   # the loss itself in float32
        opt.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
        loss.backward()                                         # gradients reach every encoder weight
        torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)
        opt.step()                                              # AdamW, peak lr 2e-5 to 5e-5
        sched.step()                                            # linear warm-up (6%), then linear decay
    val_acc = accuracy(logits_for(model, val_x, ...), val_y)    # keep the best epoch, stop early otherwise

Two details make it work well on an Apple GPU. Batches are length-bucketed: texts of similar length are trained together, so there is little padding, while the order stays random. Training runs under bfloat16 autocast, about 1.5 times faster than float32; classification runs in float32, so a text gets the same answer alone or in a batch and on the CPU.

Every file's full code is in shared/finetune.py and classifiers/finetune_deberta/.

DeBERTa-v3-base is the most accurate model in the benchmark on Banking77's common tier: 94.2%, 2.7 points above tfidf_logreg (91.5%), with all 77 labels predicted; on the whole Banking77 split it scores 93.2%. The from-scratch networks learn word meanings from our data alone and land at or below TF-IDF. The fine-tuned encoders bring their pre-training; the difference between the two groups is what pre-training buys.

Both fine-tuned encoders beat TF-IDF beyond any doubt on both tasks, and they are tied with each other. The from-scratch networks, tied with TF-IDF on the 1,000-text sample, are significantly worse than it on the whole splits. What the remaining errors are

Reading the 30 most confident errors per task: on AG News about a third are label noise ("Bryant Makes First Appearance at Trial" labelled Science/Technology, "Argentina Beats U.S. Men's Basketball Team" labelled World) and most of the rest are business stories about technology companies, which fit either label; business and sci_tech account for 207 of ModernBERT's 395 errors. On Banking77 about a third are label noise ("whats your exchange rate" labelled exchange_charge) and most of the rest are overlapping intents such as card_arrival and card_delivery_estimate. Only a handful are clear model mistakes, and the two fine-tuned models share about two thirds of their errors. At this level, better labels or label descriptions would help more than a bigger model.
